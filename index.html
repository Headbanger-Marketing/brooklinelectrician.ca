Before work begins

Check what the controls need before you buy them.

A recently built home may have boxes and cable runs the builder roughed in for switches or lighting but never finished as the plans showed, and panel labels naming circuits as planned, not as finished. Labels in any home can differ from the wiring.

Before choosing a dimmer, timer or smart device, note which switch controls which light and what the fixture instructions say about compatible controls. Read the warranty documents to see who they say should work on the wiring.

Can I keep my current fixtures if I add a dimmer later?

Not always. Compatibility depends on the fixture, its light source and the dimmer's instructions, so gather the product sheets first. Ask the contractor to confirm in writing that the chosen control suits the existing wiring and the fixtures.

Can a smart switch replace my existing switch without rewiring?

It depends on the switch box, such as whether a neutral conductor is present, and on what the device requires; only a contractor can judge that on site. Photograph the existing switch from the outside and keep the device instructions to share with the provider.

What should a quote spell out for dimmers, timers and sensors?

Request a list of every control by room from each contractor, with the circuits it touches and who sets up the app or schedule. Quotes that leave these lines out are hard to compare fairly.

Who should I ask before changing controls in a newer home?

Start with the warranty documents and any condominium or homeowner agreements, since they may say who may alter the wiring. Then ask the Licensed Electrical Contractor whether an ESA notification of work applies to the change.
